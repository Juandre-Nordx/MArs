# v0.2.1 — Infrastructure networks and visual update

Extends the Stage 2 colony merged at `c8a8e91`. No mining, manufacturing, rover traffic or trading is introduced.

## Physical network model

Water, oxygen, electrical cables and roads occupy four independent infrastructure layers on the existing 20×20 map. Segments connect only to enabled, non-broken cardinal neighbors in their own channel. Diagonals and visual proximity never connect. Different layers, including roads, may share a free terrain tile without joining logically. No infrastructure or road may occupy a building footprint, and building construction requires clearing its footprint first.

Utilities use one explicit connection tile immediately north of a building's anchor, offset `(0, -1)`. The building catalog declares only the channels it uses: solar/batteries expose electrical ports; tanks expose their resource channel; habitats expose water, oxygen and power; production facilities expose input/output channels and power. Connecting a pipe/cable to some other edge does not supply that building. Roads expose cardinal adjacency to footprint edges for future pathfinding; buildings do not bridge separate road components.

Network IDs derive deterministically from channel and first component segment. IDs may change after a split/merge. Each component records its segments, buildings, producers, storage, consumers, available supply, requested demand, actual allocations, current storage/capacity and operational status. `shortage` means supply is below demand; `blocked` means some requested consumption could not run for other reasons, such as missing feedstock. Roads expose connectivity without resource allocation or traffic.

Connectivity is rebuilt in the pure simulation module on deployment, building construction, infrastructure edits/toggles and load. Fixed ticks reuse that topology; Phaser never builds network components. Network masks/textures are refreshed on topology/view/selection changes, not on every frame. The existing fixed-step clock and speeds remain unchanged.

## Conservation and power

The HUD's water, oxygen and energy are aggregate totals of **physical per-building stores**, not globally available resources. A tank or battery retains its contents when its port is disconnected, its network splits, or a different component merges. Topology changes alone neither move reserves nor create resources. New storage starts empty. The Starship retains its cargo storage capabilities; dedicated tanks receive new-game utility cargo first.

Water and oxygen components pool only their own enabled, healthy storage. Recipes withdraw inputs from and deposit outputs into the appropriate connected component. A producer with no connection or insufficient output storage stops. Habitats draw water/oxygen only from their own channel connections; a disconnected habitat displays `No water connection` / `No oxygen connection`, and unmet needs raise critical alerts. Spare stores elsewhere do not meet those needs. There is no hydraulic pressure, flow-rate or pipe-loss model.

Each electrical component independently evaluates connected daylight solar production, local batteries and consumer demand. Integer per-building priorities (0 highest, 100 lowest) determine allocation, with deterministic ID ordering for ties. Each consumer is charged once per tick and stops if it cannot receive its full required power. Surplus charges connected enabled batteries up to their capacity; shortfalls discharge only connected batteries. Extra generation is curtailed. Disconnected or disabled battery contents remain isolated, even when the colony-wide HUD shows stored energy.

The existing recipes and population rates are retained. Food, metal, wastewater, carbon dioxide and nutrients still use Stage 2 shared logistics; these are not modeled as potable water or oxygen networks. Habitat water/oxygen consumption is passive while connected, even during an electrical outage; powered production requires electricity. Crew is assigned to occupied housing in deterministic order. Health/damage to buildings, mortality and detailed logistics remain outside this release.

## Starter connectivity and legacy colonies

New deployments include a mission-supplied utility and road blueprint. It connects all starter ports and essential buildings while avoiding footprints; the current starter layout uses 146 segments across four components. The initial resources, 19 building IDs/types/footprints and ten colonists remain unchanged. Deployment is idempotent. Players can immediately remove or extend these networks.

Existing version 2 colonies receive a one-time automatic infrastructure blueprint around their current buildings. Routing never moves or deletes a building. If a player-built footprint has enclosed a port, that port is left disconnected and reported as a fault, rather than discarding the colony. Mission/migration-supplied segments have a paid cost of zero and cannot be demolished for free metal.

## Construction and interaction

Open **Infrastructure** in the bottom toolbar and choose Water Pipe, Oxygen Pipe, Electrical Cable or Road. Left click starts a route; dragging extends it with cardinal steps. Release leaves a transparent green/red preview and shows its total material cost. **Confirm route** commits atomically. Clear, Escape or right click cancels without spending. Camera drag remains available outside placement mode; middle-button drag also pans during placement.

Toggle **Demolish tool** after choosing a layer to remove that layer along a route. Other layers on the same tiles survive. Segment inspection also supports enable/disable and single-segment removal. Zero-health or disabled segments leave the graph immediately and appear red with a fault cross; placing over such a segment replaces it at full construction cost. No random damage events are introduced, but damage/disabled state is validated and preserved in saves.

| Layer            | Metal per new/replaced tile | Demolition rule                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------: | ------------------------------------------ |
| Water pipe       |                        0.25 | 50% of actual paid metal × health fraction |
| Oxygen pipe      |                        0.25 | 50% of actual paid metal × health fraction |
| Electrical cable |                        0.50 | 50% of actual paid metal × health fraction |
| Road             |                        1.00 | 50% of actual paid metal × health fraction |

Rules live in `infrastructureDefinitions`. Existing usable same-layer segments are not charged again; repeated coordinates are deduplicated. Routes must be connected, in bounds, outside buildings, and affordable. Removal is rejected if the refund cannot fit metal storage. Replacement/refund rules cannot create a profitable build/demolish loop.

The network display selector offers Normal, Water, Oxygen, Electrical and Road views. Selected buildings highlight their real connected components; port markers show their channel colors. The inspector shows network ID, connection/fault status, supply/demand/allocated quantities, storage/capacity, member/segment counts and saved power priority. Connected input/output inventory values refer to the selected building's component, not unrelated colony stores. Supply includes starting reserves and that tick's generation; quantities reset to current-storage observations on graph rebuild and receive tick metrics on the next step.

## Artwork

`apps/web/src/art.ts` generates and caches transparent isometric textures once per Phaser scene. Building sprites are reused by all instances; roads/utilities cache all 16 cardinal neighbor masks for straight, turn, T and intersection variants. The original projection and actual footprint picking are preserved, so transparent sprite padding never becomes a selection hitbox.

Original temporary art now includes white/grey pressurized habitats, industrial cylinders/pipes, water/oxygen tanks, a covered greenhouse with planted rows, photovoltaic glass/cell grids, battery cabinets, warehouse doors/ribs, and a detailed Starship that remains the command centre. Structures share a consistent isometric view, shadows and highlights. No commercial assets are copied.

**These are explicitly temporary procedural sprites**, labeled in the UI. An artist should replace the cached building textures (including the Starship/command-centre view), refine scale/lighting/materials and supply a production atlas. Existing texture origins and catalog footprints must be retained or migrated. No external asset downloads are needed for this release.

## Save format and database

Gameplay schema advances from 2 to **3**. Saves persist all segments with type, tile, ID, health, enabled state and paid construction cost; per-building physical stores and power priorities; network rules version 1; and network observations. Ports are deterministic catalog offsets tied to the save schema/rules version, not arbitrary visual sprite positions. A future port-rule change requires a migration.

Loading validates primary state, rebuilds derived connectivity from the saved segments/ports, then validates footprints, duplicate IDs, capacities, aggregate/local reserve consistency and topology. Derived network observations are never trusted as a source of resources. Missing or stale cached components can be rebuilt safely. Version 2 migration preserves every building ID/position/condition, inventory total, seed, tick, speed and credits, allocates existing utility reserves into containers, and grants the one-time free compatibility blueprint. Reloading version 3 does not redeploy buildings or regenerate removed segments. Version 1 remains supported through its documented starter-structure treatment; it receives no extra cargo or credits. Legacy over-capacity inventories are rejected rather than silently discarded.

There is **no SQL schema change or new database migration**. The existing JSONB state and integer schema version can hold version 3. Browser saves remain the only game save interface; cloud-save/auth issuance routes are not introduced. Railway Dockerfile, Compose, port binding, static asset serving and readiness paths are unchanged. The package/workspace versions and lockfile advance to 0.2.1 with no dependency upgrades.

## Validation and limitations

- 47 unit tests cover physical connections, independent water/oxygen components, disconnected habitats/solar/batteries, charging/discharging, power priorities, split/merge conservation, route costs/crossings/refunds, broken/disabled segments, roads, save persistence/migration and the earlier simulation/economy checks.
- Seven Chromium browser tests cover drag preview/confirm/cancel, layered routes, demolition, overlay changes, immediate disconnection/alerts, invalid footprint placement, right-click cancellation, and the existing landing/colony/camera flows.
- Production workspace build, lint and formatting checks pass. Vite still reports the advisory large Phaser chunk warning.
- Compiled production server smoke passed for assets, release health/version, deployment, simulation ticks, network inspection/overlay and browser save. Layouts at 1366×768, 1280×720 and 1024×768 had no horizontal overflow.
- Full Docker/Prisma integration and a live Railway deployment remain unverified here: the current runtime has no configured live database credentials, and the Prisma binary download destination is still denied. No production service/database was modified. Run the README's database/container smoke checks in the deployment environment before publishing this release.

No pressure simulation, pipe throughput limits, infrastructure maintenance events, rover pathfinding/traffic, mining or manufacturing is claimed.
