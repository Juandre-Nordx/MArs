# v0.2.2 — Walking colonists and buggy arrivals

Extends v0.2.1 without replacing its terrain, buildings, physical utility networks, construction, landing or deployment configuration.

## Visible people

The ten starting colonists now have unique saved IDs, a home, a tile position, a previous position, a destination, a route and an activity. They walk between actual enabled buildings and their homes, with short rest periods. Movement and navigation are deterministic fixed-tick simulation; Phaser draws original cached transparent spacesuit sprites and interpolates between the simulation positions. Animation follows pause and 1×/2×/4× speed and never changes resources or population.

Routes prefer enabled roads, can use traversable bare terrain, and never enter a building footprint. Each moving person advances one tile per simulation tick. Buildings cannot be placed on a person or buggy's current tile. A blocked route is discarded and replanned on a subsequent trip. People can share tiles; traffic collision avoidance is not implemented. Walking is ambient colony life, not a replacement for the existing worker-allocation rules or a production bonus.

## Resource consumption

The original ten people were already consuming resources; making them visible does **not** charge them twice. Every registered resident continues to require, per tick:

- 0.010 kg oxygen;
- 0.008 kg water;
- 0.003 kg food.

Water and oxygen rations are withdrawn through that resident's assigned habitat's connected physical networks, including while walking in a suit. Disconnected or unhoused residents cannot draw utilities from an unrelated network; their unmet needs raise the existing alerts. Food uses the existing shared logistics. This is a simplified ration/suit-provisioning model, not an individual oxygen-tank or carried-inventory simulation. Waste recovery, shortages and storage limits retain the existing rules. Mortality remains unimplemented.

An eleventh person increases food use from 0.030 to 0.033 kg per tick, with matching per-person water/oxygen demand. New residents begin consuming on the tick after drop-off. The buggy does not grant food, water, energy, credits or construction materials.

## Attracting newcomers

1. Construct another modular housing unit. Each unit has one bed; the ten starter houses are initially full.
2. Connect the house's north-side port to water, oxygen and electrical infrastructure using the existing Infrastructure toolbar.
3. Keep existing residents housed and supplied. Immigration requires 30 ticks of food for the projected population and local water/oxygen reserves covering residents plus rated production inputs in each component. The candidate home must have connected generation covering grid demand, or 30 ticks of battery supply. Current life-support shortages and settlement power faults hold immigration.
4. Keep the terrain approach clear. A buggy chooses a traversable route from the map boundary, preferring roads; a road all the way to the edge is not required.
5. Leave arrivals enabled and the simulation running. The first eligibility check is at tick 30; unsuccessful checks repeat every 30 ticks. After drop-off or rejection, the next eligibility check is delayed 60 ticks. Only one buggy is active at a time.

The **Settlement residents** panel shows actual population, walkers and vacant beds; **Buggy arrivals** shows readiness or the blocking reason. **Pause arrivals** prevents newcomers independently of pausing simulation time. A resident roster shows current activities and homes.

The buggy is visible while driving to a specific vacant home. Population stays unchanged during travel. On reaching the door, the simulation checks housing, connections, power, resource reserves and arrival permission again. If eligible, exactly one passenger becomes a uniquely identified resident, then the empty buggy drives back. If conditions deteriorate or arrivals are paused, it turns away without adding a person. A blocked departure waits for a traversable exit rather than unloading again. The seat and target home are persisted; load never repeats a completed drop-off.

## Persistence and compatibility

Gameplay save schema advances from **3 to 4**. It saves resident identities/homes, positions, routes/activities, the next resident counter, arrival permission, check timing, last arrival event and the buggy's route/arrival-or-departure phase. Version 3 saves gain resident records for their existing population at safe building entrances. Their population, building IDs/positions, infrastructure, reserves, credits, speed and tick remain unchanged. Migration grants no new cargo and no automatic extra colonists. Versions 1 and 2 remain supported through the existing compatibility paths. Schema validation checks population/identity consistency, housing capacity, positions and cardinal routes.

No SQL migration is required: the existing JSONB colony state and integer schema version accommodate version 4. Browser Save/Load remains the save interface. Cloud saves and login issuance are not introduced. Dockerfile, Compose, Prisma schema and Railway configuration are unchanged; workspace versions/lockfile and API release health metadata advance to 0.2.2 without dependency upgrades.
